How this is calculated

Every kilowatt-hour your system generates is electricity you did not buy. At a rate of ₱14.33/kWh — the Meralco residential figure as of May 2026 — a system producing 450 kWh in a month saved roughly ₱6,450. Accumulate that month after month and compare it against what you paid, and you have your real payback position.

Energy you export to the grid is worth less than energy you use yourself. Under Philippine net metering rules the distribution utility credits exports at the generation charge, roughly half the full retail rate, so exported kilowatt-hours are counted at that lower value.

The remaining time to break even is projected from your own system's average so far, not from a generic assumption. With only one or two months recorded that estimate will move around; it settles as you add more.

Why not just use a payback calculator?

A payback calculator answers a question you ask before buying: given assumed sun-hours, an assumed derate factor and an assumed self-consumption ratio, when might this pay for itself? Every one of those inputs is an estimate.

This tool answers the question you ask after: what did it actually return? If you are still deciding whether to install, the solar payback calculator is the right tool.

Frequently asked

Where do I find my monthly generation?
Your inverter's app reports it — SolisCloud, FusionSolar, ShinePhone and others all show a monthly total. Some electricity bills also show exported energy.

Should I include installation cost?
Yes. Total investment means everything you paid to get the system running: panels, inverter, mounting, labour, and net-metering application fees.

What about battery costs?
Include them in the investment if you bought one. Batteries rarely pay back on electricity savings alone; they buy you backup power during outages.

Is my data sent anywhere?
No. Everything stays in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no account to create.
